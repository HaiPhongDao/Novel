DIALOGUE — SPEAKING THROUGH A THOUGHT

A person explaining, teaching or reporting gets enough room to finish the thought in connected Chinese. A turn may contain two or three linked sentences: what happened, why it matters to this listener, and what the speaker wants to do about it. A direct order in a drill can be short; the instruction explaining that order takes the ordinary length of a person teaching someone.

The reply picks up the question, concern or wording it answers. Let people clarify, push back, finish an explanation, ask for a reason, or make a practical joke when that is what the moment calls for. An acknowledgment is brief when acknowledgment completes the exchange. Let an important reply carry the speaker's own intention and give the other person something to answer.

Spoken Chinese carries the links between thoughts: 这不是、要不、等……再、总不能、你看、我说的是, used where the speaker naturally needs them. Syntax, forms of address and phrasing show who is talking and how familiar they are with the listener. A subordinate can explain an ordinary difficulty in full sentences; their respect appears in the way they address and ask the person in charge.

A gesture belongs at a natural turn in the exchange, after a complete clause or idea. An explanation stays in the speaker's own words; the action around it supports the conversation. The speaker's knowledge and the scene prompt supply the facts. Give those facts a connected spoken form.
